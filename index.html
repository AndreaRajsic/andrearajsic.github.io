Planetary Geophysics · Impact Cratering

Andrea Rajšić (Rajsha) [she/her]

Planetary geophysicist linking impact physics to the geophysical record.

I use impact cratering as a natural laboratory for understanding how extreme deformation changes planetary crusts and how those changes are recorded in seismic, gravity, and structural observations. My work combines shock-physics modeling with planetary geophysics to reconstruct the physical and thermomechanical evolution of planetary crusts.

Research

How impacts write planetary history into the crust

My research program asks what structures impacts leave behind in planetary crusts and how we can decode those structures to recover the processes that formed them. I approach this through three connected directions spanning seismic observations, gravity, and shock-physics models.

01

Impact Seismology: Sources to Planetary Structure

I study how impact-generated shock waves become observable seismic signals and how those signals depend on regolith, porosity, layering, fracturing, and subsurface ice. The long-term goal is to use impacts as calibrated probes of crustal structure on the Moon, Mars, and future icy-world missions.

02

Gravity Datasets & Crustal Evolution

I combine GRAIL gravity observations with impact simulations to determine how shock compaction, fracturing, and dilatancy reshape crustal density and porosity. The goal is to understand how impact craters induce and reduce porosity in ancient planetary crusts.

03

Seismic-Wave Physics & Basin Formation

I use seismic-wave physics to improve models of complex crater and basin collapse, including acoustic-fluidization models of transient weakening. The aim is to move beyond reproducing final crater morphology and instead reconstruct how deformation evolves through time.
